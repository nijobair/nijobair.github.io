Coffee Shop Sales Analysis

A decision-focused Power BI solution designed to help coffee shop management understand sales performance, identify growth opportunities, and investigate the drivers behind revenue changes.

Project Overview

This case study demonstrates how transactional coffee shop sales data can be transformed into a business-focused analytical solution.

Rather than simply displaying sales numbers, the objective was to create a dashboard that helps management understand performance, identify trends, compare stores and products, and investigate areas requiring attention.

The Business Problem

Management had access to transaction data but lacked a consolidated view of business performance.

Sales information was available at a detailed level, but it was difficult to quickly answer questions such as:

  • How are overall sales performing?
  • Which stores generate the most revenue?
  • Which products contribute most to sales?
  • How does performance change throughout the day, week, or month?
  • Which areas deserve further investigation?

The core challenge was therefore not simply to visualize the data, but to turn the data into information that supports better business decisions.

Project Objectives

The dashboard was designed around four primary objectives.

  1. Create a clear overview of overall sales performance.
  2. Identify important sales trends and patterns.
  3. Compare store and product performance.
  4. Provide a path from high-level performance to detailed analysis.

Data & Preparation

The analytical model was organized around transactional sales data and supporting dimensions such as products, stores, and calendar information.

Before building the dashboard, the data was reviewed for consistency, relationships, calculated metrics, and analytical usability.

Analytical Approach

The analysis was structured around a progression from overall performance to underlying drivers.

1. Performance

The first layer focuses on the most important business KPIs and their movement over time.

2. Comparison

Performance was then compared across stores, products, and relevant time periods.

3. Drivers

Finally, the analysis moves into the factors contributing to strong or weak performance.

Descriptive Analysis

What happened? Revenue, transactions, product performance, and store performance.

Diagnostic Analysis

Why did it happen? Trends, product mix, store differences, and time-based patterns.

Dashboard Design

The dashboard was designed around a simple analytical journey:

What happened? → How is it changing? → Where is it happening? → What is driving it?

Key Insights

The analytical process revealed several useful patterns that would otherwise be difficult to identify from raw transaction records.

  • Sales performance varied significantly across locations.
  • A relatively small group of products contributed a substantial share of revenue.
  • Sales exhibited clear time-based patterns.
  • Store-level differences created opportunities for more targeted operational decisions.

Recommendations

Based on the analysis, management could use the dashboard to focus attention on several areas.

  1. Investigate the drivers behind differences in store performance.
  2. Prioritize high-performing products while evaluating underperforming categories.
  3. Use time-based sales patterns to improve staffing and operational planning.
  4. Monitor performance regularly rather than relying only on periodic manual reports.

Outcome

Business Value

The final solution turns detailed sales transactions into a structured analytical experience that allows stakeholders to monitor performance, identify patterns, investigate drivers, and make more informed decisions.

What I Learned

The most important lesson from this project was that dashboard development is not primarily a visualization exercise.

The quality of the final dashboard depends on understanding the business problem, selecting meaningful KPIs, building a reliable analytical model, and creating a visual structure that guides users toward useful conclusions.

A successful dashboard should reduce the distance between data and decision.
